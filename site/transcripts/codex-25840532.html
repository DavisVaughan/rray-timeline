Refining axis-reduction naming preferences

I would drop _along() and give the short names to the reducers.

rray_sum(x, axes)
rray_product(x, axes)
rray_mean(x, axes)
rray_min(x, axes)
rray_max(x, axes)
rray_all(x, axes)
rray_any(x, axes)

Then give elementwise operations distinct names:

rray_minimum(x, y)
rray_maximum(x, y)

That is the split used by the original rray, and I think it was right. It also matches the useful NumPy distinction between minimum() and min().

For logical elementwise operations, rray_logical_and() and rray_logical_or() already leave rray_all() and rray_any() free for reduction.

The main reasons:

  • Reduction is the natural meaning of sum, mean, all, and any.
  • Users will expect min and max to belong to that same reducer family.
  • minimum and maximum clearly describe elementwise selection.
  • The name does not need to say “along” when the axes argument already says it.
  • It avoids letting the naming needs of two functions make every reducer longer.

I would also consider axes = NULL to mean all axes:

rray_sum(x)       # Reduce over every axis
rray_sum(x, 1L)   # Reduce over axis 1

This works especially well with rray4’s rule that reductions keep dimensionality. rray_sum(x) can return an array whose dimensions are all 1, while integer() can continue to mean “reduce over no axes.”

I would not use mixed naming such as rray_sum() but rray_min_along(). That makes the family depend on accidental name clashes. I also would not overload rray_min(x, y) and rray_min(x, axes = ...); numeric y and numeric axes make that inherently ambiguous.

If you strongly want rray_min() for the current elementwise operation, I would keep _along() on every reducer. Uniformly verbose is better than selectively terse. In that design, though, _along should mark a real conceptual category, not just solve two collisions.

My preferred final vocabulary is therefore:

Operation Name
Reduce over axes rray_sum(), rray_min(), rray_any()
Elementwise extrema rray_minimum(), rray_maximum()
Elementwise logical rray_logical_and(), rray_logical_or()

That gives the most common operations the cleanest names and makes the whole family predictable.
