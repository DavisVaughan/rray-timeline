Short answer: I'd do it for the functions where positional order is genuinely ambiguous, not as a blanket rule.

Why not blanket. Most of your API takes exactly one argument after x, and the function name already names it:

rray_permute_axes(x, axes = c(2, 1))
rray_sum_along(x, axes = 1)
rray_broadcast(x, dimensions = c(2, 3))

That is a stutter. "axes" appears twice in the same call and the reader learned nothing. These are also the ones that show up in pipes and loops, so the tax is paid constantly to fix a problem that does not exist there.

Where it does pay. The functions with two trailing arguments of the same shape, where nothing at the call site tells you which is which:

rray_split(x, 1, c(1, 5))       # which is the axis?
rray_move_axes(x, 1, 2)         # from or to first?
rray_set_axis_names(x, 1, nms)

Your own rray_split() examples are the proof. rray_split(x, 2, 1) is unreadable without the help page. That's four or so functions total, which is why I don't think it justifies a rule over the whole package.

Two things in favour of your version, to be fair. A blanket rule needs no memory: "everything after x/y is named" is one sentence and there's never a lookup. And the direction is safe. Going from forced-named to positional later is not breaking (f(x, ..., axis) → f(x, axis, ...) keeps every named call working), while the reverse is. So if you're unsure, forced-named is the reversible choice.

On mechanism. Forcing names means ... + check_dots_empty0() right after x. That conflicts with the functions where ... is real data:

rray_max(x, y, ..., na_rm = FALSE)
rray_combine(..., .axis)

Those already force naming via the dot prefix. So a blanket rule doesn't actually give you one uniform look, you end up with axis = in one place and .axis = in another. That weakens the consistency argument that motivates it.

What I'd do: force naming on rray_split(), rray_move_axes(), rray_set_axis_names(), and rray_broadcast_names2(); leave the single-argument functions positional; keep arg/call developer arguments after ... as you already have in rray_cast().

Notes

Three changes out of 39. All three are the same shape: two required arguments after x that are both integer vectors, so the call site gives the reader nothing.

Five functions already force naming through ..., either with a dot prefix (.axis, .dimensions) or with real dots ahead of na_rm. Adding these three brings the total to eight, and the other 31 have at most one thing to name.

One cost to know about: ... ahead of a required argument means you need check_dots_empty0(...), and a forgotten argument reports as missing later than it would now. Worth it for these three, not worth it for rray_broadcast(x, c(2, 3)).

The non-exported helpers (rray_cast(), rray_ptype2(), rray_ptype_common(), rray_broadcast_names2(), rray_reduce_names()) are not in the table. rray_broadcast_names2(x, y, dimensions) would qualify if you ever export it.

      indices <- lapply(x_dimensions, seq_len)
